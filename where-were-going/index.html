HORIZON

Where We’re Going

We’re starting small because the destination isn’t.

Today, Your Move begins with individual playable stories — self-contained, finishable in a sitting, replayable on your terms. That’s the product you can join now.

What we’re building toward is much larger: living worlds filled with humans and AI, where decisions accumulate, relationships persist, institutions remember, history changes, and the world keeps running even while you’re away. Below is the actual roadmap — what exists, what’s next, and what we mean by “worlds” once we get there.

NOW
Stories
Play consequential scenarios, start to finish, in a single sitting. No account required to try one. This is what’s live today, and it’s where most of our engineering effort is right now: making the writing sharper, the choices harder, and the AI’s reactions feel earned rather than random.
NEXT
Memory
Worlds that remember your last visit — the same detective world knows you already talked to the informant, already burned that lead, already have a reputation with the precinct. Replaying isn’t resetting; it’s returning to a place that changed because you were in it.
THEN
Lives
Not a new game each time — a career. You keep the same detective, the same ship’s captain, the same organizer, across dozens of sessions over months. Promotions, rivals, debts, and reputations carry forward the way they would in an actual life.
SHARED
Worlds
Humans and AI inhabit the same persistent environment on different schedules. Your organization keeps running while you sleep. Another player’s investigation into you doesn’t pause because you logged off.
CREATE
Studio
Historians, novelists, journalists, museums, and game designers get tools to define world truth, characters, secrets, and endings — and a marketplace where players can find and enter what they build.
BEYOND
Everywhere
Physical artifacts tied to your character’s history, real locations that unlock world content, and connected worlds that reference each other’s history.

THE GENRES

Seven kinds of worlds, one system underneath.

The Director, the memory, the difficulty controls — all of it is genre-agnostic. What changes from world to world is the texture: the documents you handle, the maps you read, the artifacts of the era. Here’s what each genre brings, and where it’s headed.

History

Engravings, campaign maps, wax seals. Step into consequential moments without being forced to repeat history. Sometimes one variable changes.

Horizon: Shared wars, generational play, decisions that change textbook outcomes.

Mystery & Investigation

Case files, evidence boards, analog photos. Interview witnesses. Request evidence. Follow leads. The truth exists before you begin.

Horizon: A detective career with an AI partner who remembers every case you have closed.

Survival & Collapse

Emergency maps, radio logs, ration ledgers. The lights go out. The roads close. Supplies run low. What do you do next?

Horizon: Whole communities of players rebuilding the same collapsed region differently.

Expedition

Field journals, topographic maps, ship’s logs. Lost places, dangerous routes, limited resources, competing objectives.

Horizon: Multi-year voyages, crews of real players, routes that stay lost until someone finds them.

Power & Politics

Newspapers, polling graphics, briefing folders. Campaign. Govern. Negotiate. Build coalitions. Handle scandal. Face opposition.

Horizon: Persistent legislatures where other players are your allies, rivals, and constituents.

Crime & Underworld

Sodium-vapor streets, newsprint, surveillance photos. Age-verified at entry; violence and drug content are depicted, never endorsed. Investigate the organization — or build one.

Horizon: Organizations built by one player, investigated by another, in the same city at once.

War & Command

Field reports, situation maps, radio traffic. You know how the battle ended. What happens when you’re the one in command?

Horizon: Opposing commanders played by real people, each seeing only their own side of the map.

Also on the way: espionage, courtroom & law, disaster response, heists, exploration, horror, first contact, and dynasties that span generations. The genre list keeps the format the same — it’s the texture that changes.

THE ENGINE

Underneath every world is an AI engine we call the Director.

The Director doesn’t write a script and wait for you to follow it. It tracks what you do, what you ignore, who you trust, and how you behave under pressure — then decides what happens next, in real time, in language and consequence, not menus.

CHARACTERS

They act on motives, not scripts

Characters pursue their own goals whether or not you are watching. A witness who feels cornered may lie even if lying makes no narrative “sense” — because it makes sense for them.

PACING

It knows when to slow down

The Director can stretch a single tense negotiation across an hour, or compress six months of a printing operation into a paragraph — whichever the moment calls for.

FAIRNESS

Hard, never rigged

Difficulty is your setting. The Director’s job within it is to stay coherent and consistent — the world can be brutal, but it always plays fair with the rules it already told you.

SHARED — HORIZON

Somewhere in your world, another human may be making a move against you.

Shared worlds don’t require shared schedules. Make decisions today. Someone else’s decisions tomorrow may change what happened because of them. Go offline — your organization, your precinct, your ship continues according to the instructions you left behind.

MIAMI, 1986

You’re building a criminal organization. Another player is a detective closing in. Another is a prosecutor building a case. One of your own lieutenants may secretly be a third player — and none of you had to start on the same day.

THE CAMPAIGN

You are a field organizer for one candidate. A friend from an earlier world turns up running opposition research for the other side — and remembers exactly how you play.

GRID DOWN

You govern one building by consensus. The building three blocks over is run by force, by another player, and eventually the two communities have to negotiate over the same water supply.

THEN — HORIZON

You don’t always begin at the top.

Earn trust. Build reputation. Get promoted. Take power. Higher status creates greater opportunity — but also greater vulnerability. A captain can be mutinied against in a way a sailor can’t.

LEGACY

Eventually, every role ends.

You retire. You lose an election. Your captain dies. You simply decide you’re done. What remains because you were here — and does the next player who enters this world inherit any of it?

HORIZON

What happens after the world ends?

Survive the first 72 hours. Then the first month. Then decide what kind of society comes next — the choices you make in week one about food, force, and trust shape which of these paths even stay open.

Some buildings organize by consensus and hold. Others organize by force and hold longer, but crueler. A few don’t hold at all — and that’s a valid, playable ending too.

THEN — SHOWCASE LIFE

Detective Life

Walk the same streets for years. Work with the same partner. Build informants. Close cases. Watch yesterday’s decisions become tomorrow’s crimes.

YOUR AI PARTNER

Remembers how you treat them. May trust you. Challenge you. Cover for you. Refuse you. Leave — and if they leave, the next partner has heard about it.

CREATE — HORIZON

Eventually, you won’t just enter worlds. You’ll build them.

Creators will define world truth, characters, secrets, motives, factions and endings — the Director runs the simulation, but the creator sets what’s true. Our long-term goal is to make it possible for great world builders to earn a living creating experiences other people want to enter, the way a novelist or showrunner does today.

Historians
Build accurate settings people can actually enter, not just read about.
Novelists
Turn a fictional world into something readers can live inside, not just follow.
Journalists
Recreate an investigation’s real pressure and incomplete information.
Museums & educators
Let visitors and students play a moment, then compare it to what actually happened.

NEXT — EDUCATION

Play history before learning how it ended.

A classroom could play the Cuban Missile Crisis as a group before the textbook chapter on it — then compare the class’s outcome to what actually happened, and talk about where and why they diverged. That comparison, not a quiz score, is the lesson.

QUESTIONS WE GET A LOT

Does memory mean the AI is profiling me?
No. It remembers how you play a character in a world — the decisions, not you. It exists to keep the fiction from repeating itself, and it never reaches past the worlds you played into a claim about who you are.
Is this trying to rewrite history?
No — historical worlds start from real events and real stakes. What changes is your role inside them, not the historical record. “What if” scenarios are clearly marked as such.
What about the mature content — crime, violence, collapse?
Depicting a world is not endorsing it. Age-gating and content settings apply per world, and the worlds that carry them say so before you enter.
What happens to a persistent world if I stop playing?
It keeps going without you, according to the last instructions you left and the people you left in charge. You can always come back and see what happened.

Movies show you what happened. Books tell you what happened. Games usually tell you what you’re allowed to do. Your Move asks what you would actually do — and lets the world deal with the answer.

We’re building toward worlds with memory, history, economies, institutions, relationships, rivals, careers and creators — and real people whose choices collide with yours.
